---
title: "Lightness illusions"
excerpt: "There are a huge number of lightness illusions that typically make targets look lighter or darker than expected in apparently unpredictable ways"
themes: []
---

Most illusions shown can be classed as simultaneous contrast, assimilation (White's illusion) or crispening (which includes the flip between the two). Additional illusions shown here are the Chevreul staircase, where a lightness-step grid shown in line with a matched smooth gradient appears as a series of gradients. And the Hermann grid, where black dots appear at the intersection of a grid of black squares.

The SBL model is able qualitatively to predict all these illusions. i.e. it can predict whether a target will appear lighter or darker than expected from the input. This is impressive given the model is purely feed-forward (e.g. no divide-by local value loops). The way it achieves this can be understood when investigating the patterns of sub-threshold and saturation contrasts at different spatial frequencies.
