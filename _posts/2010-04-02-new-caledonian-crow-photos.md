Here are some of the photos I’ve caught of New Caledonian Crows. I worked on this species’ amazing tool-using abilities for my PhD, investigating how their morphology and behaviour have evolved to enable such impressive tool-use.

![New Caledonian Crow tool use](/assets/images/posts/new-caledonian-crow-photos/NCC-with-Tool-150x150.jpg)

NC crow with a stick tool

![New Caledonian Crow tool use](/assets/images/posts/new-caledonian-crow-photos/NCC-with-larva-150x150.jpg)

New Caledonian Crow that has extracted a Cerambycid longhorn beetle larva from the deadwood using a stick tool

![New Caledonian Crow tool use](/assets/images/posts/new-caledonian-crow-photos/NCC-probing-150x150.jpg)

New Caledonian Crow probing for larvae with a stick tool

![New Caledonian Crow tool use](/assets/images/posts/new-caledonian-crow-photos/NCC-Crowcam-150x150.jpg)

New Caledonian Crow with a crow-cam attached to the tail, looking between its legs

![](/assets/images/posts/new-caledonian-crow-photos/NCC-crowcam-diagram-150x150.jpg)

Illustration of the mounting method used for crow-cams

![](/assets/images/posts/new-caledonian-crow-photos/CrowTube-150x150.jpg)

This shot of an NC crow probing into a tube shows how their eyes can swivel forward. This photo also won in it’s category at the BBSRC’s photo competition
